Qingzhu: A Tactile Focus Companion

Qingzhu moves the start of a focus session from a phone menu onto the desk. The device handles immediate actions; the app handles settings and records.

Year
2026
Role
Core team member

Research question

Can starting a focus session feel as direct as striking a switch while still giving clear feedback?

My contribution

Core team member

  1. 01Co-defined tap-to-start, dial selection, and touch feedback
  2. 02Mapped focus contexts, personas, and design goals
  3. 03Separated device and mobile responsibilities
  4. 04Produced product, scenario, and interface visuals

01 · Literature & cases

Most focus tools keep interaction on the screen

Early research compared digital focus tools, desk timers, and companion devices. They can manage tasks, but often send the user back to a phone to start, switch, or review a session. This set the boundary: core actions should leave the phone, while complex settings can remain in the app.

02 · Context & experience

The friction sits in starting, resuming, and checking

Scenarios and personas separated the focus process into starting a task, resuming after interruption, and checking progress. Each moment needs quick confirmation rather than a full menu, so the desktop device should handle immediate actions.

03 · Interviews & analysis

The evidence frames a direction but does not validate demand

The portfolio documents case comparison, scenario analysis, and personas, but not reproducible interview samples, survey statistics, or task data. These materials support an initial direction; they do not prove that tapping is more effective.

Later interviews should examine interruption recovery, phone dependence, and acceptance of physical input. A survey can show distributions but cannot replace task testing.

04 · Insights & questions

The question shifted from more features to a more direct start

The synthesis moved the project away from adding management features. The question became: can a focus session start as directly as striking a switch while making system state clear? This led to tap-to-start, dial selection, immediate feedback, and a division between device and app.

05 · Concepts & prototypes

The device handles action; the app handles configuration

Tapping, the dial, and the device screen handle starting, switching, and confirmation. Whitelists, modes, and history remain on the phone. This reduces device menus and creates a feedback hierarchy: the device confirms the action first; the app provides full information.

06 · Testing & feedback

Current checks focus on interface consistency and desk scale

Current checks use interface states, product proportion, and scenario renders to examine internal consistency. No task-based user test has been completed, so learnability, false activation, and long-term acceptance remain unknown.

07 · Refinement & conclusion

The concept is coherent; the core interaction remains untested

The proposal defines the relationship between the desk device and the app and establishes a recognizable product language. The next step is not more styling, but a repeatable working prototype comparing tapping with a conventional button for first-time learning, false activation, and interruption recovery.
