Wings of the Hive: A Kinetic Window

Wings of the Hive draws opening actions from irises, origami, and honeycomb arrays, then works through synchronization, collision, and assembly with unit models, linkages, and servos.

Year
2025
Role
Project lead

Research question

How can a set of panels open together without colliding?

My contribution

Project lead

  1. 01Led concept, structure, and 3D modeling
  2. 02Developed panel folding, linkage, and actuation
  3. 03Tested units and arrays across iterations
  4. 04Fabricated, assembled, and tuned the physical prototype

01 · Literature & cases

Irises, origami, and honeycombs offered three movement cues

The review did not copy natural forms directly. It extracted relationships useful for mechanism design: radial motion from the iris, surface-and-fold logic from origami, and repeatable arrays from honeycombs. Together they bounded the concept space.

02 · Context & experience

A kinetic surface must balance opening, shading, and visual change

The concept treats each panel as an architectural-surface unit whose opening area changes while the array remains continuous. No real building-site study was conducted, so ventilation, shading, and scale remain design assumptions rather than environmental findings.

03 · Interviews & analysis

This project relies on structural analysis rather than user statistics

The portfolio contains no interviews, surveys, or statistical material. Analysis instead focused on panels, fixtures, linkages, gears, servos, and supports. Component decomposition examined pivots, linkage length, assembly order, and movement limits.

04 · Insights & questions

The central conflict is linkage, collision, and buildability

Early analysis showed that opening alone was not enough. Multiple units also had to move together, avoid collision, and remain buildable with available materials and fabrication. The question became: how can a set of panels open together without interfering with one another?

05 · Concepts & prototypes

The design converged from a honeycomb array to a three-branch cluster

Successive prototypes changed surfaces, pivots, linkages, and array logic. Each iteration isolated one main conflict, while discarded options remained as comparative evidence. The final three-branch cluster better supported linkage and spatial expansion.

06 · Testing & feedback

Motion studies and fabrication tested the mechanism together

Motion studies checked collision during closing, rotation, extension, and opening. Fabrication then exposed material thickness, servo placement, linkage length, and assembly tolerances. Findings informed structural changes, but cycle life, noise, and synchronization error were not quantified.

07 · Refinement & conclusion

The prototype opens; conclusions at architectural scale remain limited

The final prototype shows that the opening relationship can work, but it does not establish reliability at architectural scale. The next stage should measure synchronization error, noise, load, and cycle life before adding environmental sensing or a larger array.
